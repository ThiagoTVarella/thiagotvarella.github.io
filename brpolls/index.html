Methodology

Data

Every national poll of Lula vs Flávio with fieldwork from 14 Aug 2026, checked against the pollster's own release or a major outlet and matched to its TSE registration number. Seeded from Wikipedia; polls Wikipedia lacks were found through the TSE registry. Two polls remain unconfirmed (you can exclude them above).

Average

Each poll's weight = pollster rating × √(sample size, capped at 5,000) × recency (halves every half-life days) × a penalty for pollsters publishing many polls close together. Pollster house effects are estimated jointly with the trend and removed; they are measured against the consensus, not the truth, so a miss shared by all pollsters is invisible to the average. The quantity is Lula's share of the two-candidate vote from total-vote figures, so undecided voters drop out.

Pollster ratings

Final-three-weeks polls from 2010–2026 presidential races and 2014/2018/2022/2026 governor races, scored on the error of the margin between the top two finishers against official TSE results. Presidential races count fully, the eight largest states 0.8, the rest 0.4. Ratings are shrunk toward the average; pollsters with a short record are unrated. Ibope is treated as Ipec and FSB as Nexus.

Does the first-round miss carry over?

In every election since 2010 final first-round polls overstated the PT candidate's margin (by 13, 15, 5, 2 and 4 points in 2010, 2014, 2018, 2022 and 2026), yet runoff polls were close to unbiased (+0.3, 0.0, −4.9 and +0.2 in 2010–2022). First-round misses come largely from late switching between candidates, which a two-way runoff lacks. About a third of a pollster's miss relative to its peers reappears in the runoff, which moves the 2026 average by only a few hundredths of a point. Use “Assume polls are off by” above to stress-test a repeat of the full miss (−1.7 pts).

Forecast and backtest

Lula's share on election day = the average + a shared, fat-tailed polling error (Student t, 4 degrees of freedom, standard deviation 2 points by default) + drift for the days left + the average's own noise. The browser uses a closed-form version of the published 200,000-draw simulation, so it can differ from it by about a point of probability.
