Trend

Dots: each poll's Lula share of the two-candidate vote (total votes, undecided removed). Solid line: weighted average after correcting each pollster's lean relative to the others. Dashed line: the same, also corrected for each pollster's miss in the 2026 first round. 50% = tie.

Pollster lean and rating

Lean: how far a pollster runs from the consensus, in points of Lula's share (negative = favours Flávio). Rating: accuracy against official results in past elections (lower plus-minus is better).

How pollsters did in the 2026 first round

Error of the Flávio-minus-Lula margin in each pollster's final-10-day polls against the official result (Flávio 47.0%, Lula 45.2% of valid votes). Negative = the poll understated Flávio. Few polls per pollster, so read loosely.

Methodology

Data. Every national poll of Lula vs Flávio with fieldwork from 14 Aug 2026, checked against the pollster's own release or a major outlet and matched to its TSE registration number. Seeded from Wikipedia; a few polls Wikipedia lacks were found through the TSE registry. Two polls remain unconfirmed. Field dates differ by a day in places between sources.

Average. Each poll is weighted by pollster rating, sample size (square root, capped at 5,000), recency (halves every 8 days) and a penalty for pollsters that publish many polls close together. Pollster lean is estimated jointly with the trend and removed. Lean is measured against the consensus, not the truth, so a miss shared by all pollsters is invisible to the average.

Ratings. Final-3-weeks polls from 2014-2026 presidential races and 2014/2018/2022/2026 governor races, scored on the error of the margin between the top two finishers against official TSE results, with presidential races at full weight, the eight largest states at 0.8, and the rest at 0.4. Ratings are shrunk toward the average and are rough: no usable 2002/2006 polls, thin 2014 data, some Wikipedia tables are inconsistent, and pollsters with a short record are marked unrated. Ibope is treated as Ipec and FSB as Nexus.

The consensus problem. The average above is centred on the pollsters' consensus. In the first round, the pollsters that leaned toward Flávio (Gerp, Futura, Veritá, Palver) landed close to the official result, while Datafolha, Quaest, Nexus, MDA and Indexa understated him by 5 to 8 points of margin. A consensus-centred average therefore risks inheriting that miss. The adjusted estimate corrects each pollster by half of its own first-round miss (a judgement call: how much a miss carries into the runoff is unknown, and each pollster has only 1 to 9 polls to judge).

Forecast. 200,000 simulations of Lula's share: the average plus a shared polling error (fat-tailed, standard deviation 2 points, a judgement call because only two past runoffs had enough final-week polls) plus a little drift. Both the plain and the adjusted average are simulated. Which is closer to the truth is the main uncertainty.
