Really interesting read about Agnes Callard's uni-context. One thing I was wondering is how/whether the uni-context counterintuitively increases polarization. One idea I had goes like this: if you have everyone's opinions spread on a single line, if you split this line in two and have two marks at 25% and 75% of the mark, everyone will be at max a 25% away from that mark. So while the uni-context gives access to all of the points in the mark, including the ones right at your opinion, polarizing into two marks ensures you can get a compromise not too far from your own opinion, and that is shared with about 50% of the population. This thought needs better formalization but anyway, just putting it out there in case I (or anyone) want to formalize it further at some point.
